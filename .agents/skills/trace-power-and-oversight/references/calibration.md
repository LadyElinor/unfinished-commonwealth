# Worked comparisons

Use these to calibrate the scope of a finding. They are not a representative sample.

**A missed warning — JPMorgan / Madoff.** The bank’s London staff shared concerns with some U.S. legal and investment-bank compliance staff. They did not notify the U.S. Bank Secrecy Act officer or anti-money-laundering operations. The bank also redeemed approximately $275 million of its own hedge investments. Record the actual recipients, the omitted recipients and the redemption separately. The bank admitted a violation; that does not establish that each employee intended to assist Madoff. [FinCEN assessment, January 2014: discussion of the London reports and U.S. recipients](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/JPMorgan_ASSESSMENT_01072014.pdf).

**An intentional restriction — Ilopango.** North’s September 20, 1985 letter asked Rodríguez to help arrange resupply, specified whom not to inform and asked that the letter be destroyed. Another official’s October notes corroborate the contact arrangements. The letter supports intent to restrict circulation of this request. The notes do not prove that the letter was destroyed or that a superior approved the request in advance. Before describing secrecy as misconduct, establish the duty applicable to that act. [Walsh report, chapter 29, “North Recruits Rodriguez,” notes 43–49 and conclusion](https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/walsh/chap_29.htm).

**A disclosure with a judicially tested consequence — McIntyre.** The First Circuit affirmed civil liability against the United States after agent Connolly disclosed enough information for Bulger and Flemmi to identify McIntyre as an informant. The trial court found his killing foreseeable. Foreseeability does not establish that Connolly shared the killers’ purpose or that the FBI ordered the killing. [McIntyre v. United States, No. 07-1663, opening and conclusion](https://www.ca1.uscourts.gov/sites/ca1/files/opnfiles/07-1663P-01A.pdf).

**A working check — fictional payment desk.** On Monday a reviewer acknowledged a duplicate-invoice alert before release and ordered a hold; the bank ledger confirms no transfer. On Tuesday the same desk received a second alert. The case file has no acknowledgment and the payment cleared. Record Monday as a timely intervention. For Tuesday, record the payment and the missing receipt. If alerts can be handled by phone, obtain the duty log before saying anyone ignored the warning. Different deadlines, exemptions or authority could defeat the match. Neither two days nor a collection selected for its failures supplies an agency-wide rate.
