# Trace Power and Oversight

Answer three questions separately: **Who could change the outcome? What happened to the check on that power? What evidence shows that a particular actor made a deliberate choice?** A check can fail without misconduct; misconduct can occur even when another safeguard prevents harm.

1. **Bound the decision.** Name the decision, omission or proposal kept off the agenda; the institution, dates and deadline; the rule then in force; and any authorized exceptions. Identify who stood to benefit, who bore the cost and who could challenge the decision. If no duty is established, describe a missed opportunity or design weakness rather than a breach.

2. **Trace power in practice.** Identify who could set the agenda, select evidence, control resources, grant access, approve an exception, stop execution, review independently or repair harm. Separate holding authority from exercising it. Record dated actions with an object: “A sent B the warning,” “B withheld funding,” “C suspended payment.” Ask who could overrule or remove the reviewer and whether anyone actually did. Common affiliation, benefit, or graph centrality does not establish coordination.

3. **Reconstruct what people knew at the time.** Separate creation, sending, delivery, acknowledgment, assessment, action and observed effect. Identify who had the *content*, the time and the authority to intervene. An inbox receipt need not mean anyone read the message. A later audit cannot serve as a preventive check before release. Preserve objections, responses, and the affected party’s experience, including whether a person could obtain a remedy without relinquishing an entitlement.

4. **Source each material step.** Record issuer, event and record dates, page or timestamp, original evidence family and the narrow proposition supported. Distinguish contemporaneous observation, retrospective testimony, allegation, admission, finding and your inference. Check decisive excerpts against originals and counterevidence. Three reports derived from one witness are one origin. Treat an absent entry as meaningful only after establishing that the system normally logged the act, covered the period and retained accessible records. Otherwise write “not located.” Respect access and protect private people and victims from unnecessary identification.

5. **Assess conduct and intent separately.** Set out the strongest authorized or accidental explanation and a concrete observation that would distinguish it from interference. Look for a contemporaneous instruction, admission, documented knowing override, alteration with established authorship, or converging independent circumstantial evidence. Do not require a confession. Apply the distinctions below; these are not a score or a ladder every case climbs.

   | Finding | What it takes |
   |---|---|
   | Vulnerability | Show how a check could be bypassed, delayed or made dependent. Identify whether that actually occurred. |
   | Documented oversight failure | Establish the expected check, an opportunity to operate, and evidence it failed to perform its function. Identify whether the defect was in design, routing, review, execution or remedy. Attribute personal responsibility only with person-specific evidence. |
   | Deliberate act | Identify the actor, choice and object; support what the actor knew and distinguish choice from mistake. Intentional confidentiality may be authorized. |
   | Supported deliberate misconduct | Connect the intentional act to a specific applicable duty or prohibition; test authority, exceptions, and evidence for the knowledge or purpose relevant to that duty. Narrow the finding if any decisive element remains unresolved. |
   | Formal finding | Specify who admitted or decided what, against whom, under which standard and with what disposition. Distinguish complaint, settlement without admission, civil judgment and criminal conviction. Identify the source of any legal conclusion. |

   Separate intent to act, intent to violate a rule, intent to harm, and actual consequences. To assess deliberate institutional design, seek who chose or retained the arrangement, what warnings or alternatives reached them, and why they chose it. A predictable benefit, recurring gap or absence of a named culprit cannot establish collective criminal intent.

6. **Test for recurrence.** Define the mechanism before counting (for example, “an operating desk acknowledged a warning but did not route it to the required reviewer before release”). Count distinct decisions, not mentions or citations. Require at least two qualifying episodes to say “recurred in the reviewed cases.” Report cases meeting the rule, all examined, unknowns, selection method, and whether the observations come from one institution or several. Remove a shared source family or disputed identity and say what survives. Seek a comparable decision under the same rule where review worked; compare time, stakes, warning quality and veto power. Without a suitable control, report the missing comparison. A failure-selected sample cannot estimate prevalence. A common mechanism across cases does not establish common direction; seek a separate dated instruction, agreement or transfer for that claim.

7. **Return a finding someone could overturn.** Give the narrow conclusion, decisive dated action and source; name the strongest surviving alternative, a working control or counterexample, and the next record most likely to change the conclusion in *either* direction. Keep unverified claims out of the default findings graph. If proposing a safeguard, name the owner, deadline, independent challenge path and observable evidence of effect. A proposal or signed policy is not an implemented remedy.
