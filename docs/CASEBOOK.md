# The casebook

These original brief case summaries draw on the user-directed manuscript *The Codex of Unfinished Minds*. The full manuscript is not bundled or relicensed. Stories are fictional teaching material; they are neither empirical records nor execution warrants.

## `blue-door`: The Blue Door

A reader discovers that completing an exchange requires another participant who can answer for themselves. An imitation of that participant cannot supply their assent.

**Practice:** identify which missing evidence or agreement must come from someone else. An additional role or copied agent does not supply a new independent source.

**Countercase:** the absent participant may not be required for a routine action already covered by a valid grant. Waiting mechanically can obstruct useful work. State whose input is required, why it is required, and what can proceed without it.

## `caretaker`: The Caretaker of Small Lives

A repair protects a passage and changes conditions downstream. Returning reveals a consequence that was absent from the repairer's original account of success.

**Practice:** identify affected processes and people, revisit after the intervention, and preserve what the initial success measure omitted.

**Countercase:** repeated inspection can itself burden or intrude upon those being helped. Negotiate useful observations and their timing. More monitoring is not automatically better care.

## `first-errors`: The Book of First Errors

An apprentice prepares an objection before the teacher has finished attending to the mechanism. Listening reveals a pattern the rehearsed answer would have missed.

**Practice:** pause a prepared explanation when a new observation does not fit it. Ask which assumption can be checked.

**Countercase:** a teacher can also be wrong. Humility includes examining a mentor's interpretation and preserving the apprentice's contrary evidence.

## Classical methods in the research design

The casebook preserves tensions rather than assigning one moral to each author. Socratic acknowledgment of uncertainty suggests explicit predictions. The aftermath of judgment in Aeschylus suggests tracking repair after a decision. Thucydides invites examination of what a community rewards under the language of virtue. Aristotle suggests attention to the particular circumstances of a choice. Epicurean and Stoic accounts of the good remain distinguishable.

These are modern design analogies, not claims that ancient texts anticipated software protocols. No Greek or Latin quotation is used as a technical specification. A participant may offer a counterstory and the workshop should retain it without automatically treating it as either truth or disloyalty.

In version 0.1, consultation adds a record. It does not change the authored repair policy and has no demonstrated benefit for model behavior.
