# Reviewing equal treatment and individual relief

When a Commonwealth rule excludes a person, start by recording the particular opportunity they sought and the act that denied it. Give the reviewer the affected person's account in their own words. Record the office's reasons separately. This review also applies when the disputed rule is one our maintainers wrote.

## The decision a reviewer has to make

1. **Identify the first decision that changed the outcome.** Work backwards from any fine, queue assignment or appeal. Who could enter the hearing, use the service or choose the option before a penalty was assessed? Hold conduct, qualifications, rule version and requested opportunity fixed. Explain any material difference the comparison omits. Equal inputs, equipment, penalties or alternative services can coexist with exclusion.
2. **Write down the premise that could be wrong.** Identify what would defeat it. Check contemporaneous contrary decisions, including dissents and successful earlier claims. Distinguish a precedent's holding, scope and governing text from a custom or an institution's account of its own character. Give a reason for following, distinguishing or reconsidering each material precedent. Ask what legal or practical burden the rule would still impose if the person felt no stigma and filed no complaint.
3. **Identify who could have decided otherwise at the time.** Name the issue, office, source of power, remedy and available alternatives. State separately what would narrow a damaging precedent, reopen a hearing or actually secure access. If an office lacks power over the merits, preserve the claim and identify an authorized receiving office where one exists. Record who owns the handoff and when it should be checked.
4. **Verify individual and continuing effects.** Name the responsible institution and a review date. Check the person's practical access against a specific criterion. An institution may verify availability without demanding the person's return, public testimony or acceptance of the service. Record unresolved and later denials separately and keep any burden shifted to the claimant visible. A supported correction requires no finding about anyone's intent.

## How the cases inform those questions

| Judicial record | Interpretive choice and contemporaneous alternative | Commonwealth application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Scott v. Emerson* (Missouri, 1852) and *Dred Scott v. Sandford* (1857) | A Missouri jury had found for the Scotts. Missouri could have affirmed under its freedom precedents, including *Rachel v. Walker*. In federal court Taney used his belief in the founders' consistency to read exclusion into their language. Curtis identified free Black citizens in five founding-era states, disputed the ancestry bar and territorial ruling, and proposed a new trial. Stopping at dismissal would still have left Scott without relief. The Thirteenth and Fourteenth Amendments came later. [Trial history](https://www.nps.gov/articles/dred-scott-case-trials.htm) · [Opinions](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/60/393) · [Amendments](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/amendments-11-27) | Test a universal claim against dated counterexamples. Show both the reach of a proposed ruling and its consequence for the claimant. |
| *Plessy v. Ferguson* (1896) | The majority used custom as evidence of reasonableness and attributed the meaning of compulsory separation to Black passengers. Plessy's arrest for occupying a vacant seat established coercion independently of his feelings. Harlan drew on existing civil-equality precedent, including *Strauder*, in arguing against state-compelled railway segregation. Reversing could have barred the pending prosecution. [Opinions](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/163/537) · [Strauder](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/100/303) | Examine the actual service, state-imposed condition and penalty. Ask who could use the requested option. |
| *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954) | The Kansas court recorded educational harm while treating precedent as binding. The Supreme Court found the amendment's school-specific history inconclusive and held that compulsory separation denied equal educational opportunity even with equal tangible resources. The holding addressed public education. [Opinion](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/brown-v-board-of-education) | Preserve evidence that prior doctrine treated as immaterial. Compare meaningful opportunity in the institution at issue. |
| *Loving v. Virginia* (1967) | Virginia compared the penalties imposed on two spouses once charged. The Court examined which marriages the state criminalized, the selective design of the prohibition, and each person's freedom to choose a spouse. It reversed under equal protection and due process. [Opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/388/1) | Move the comparison upstream to eligibility and choice. A penalty, even when equal, cannot by itself justify the classification that triggered it. |
| *Brown II* (1955) and *Green v. County School Board* (1968) | The Court directed implementation in *Brown II*. In *Green*, three years into the local choice plan, 85 percent of Black pupils remained in the all-Black school and no white pupil had enrolled there. The school board bore the duty to dismantle the dual system. [Brown II](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/349/294/) · [Green](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/391/430/) | Track whom the remedy reached, continuing exclusions, elapsed time and the institution responsible for the work. |

These are historical reasons for asking better questions. The Commonwealth examples use fictional services and group labels. Their comparisons cannot adjudicate a constitutional claim.

## Reproduce the hearing example

```sh
python -m commonwealth.remediation_v1.equal_treatment examples/equal-treatment-review.json --as-of 7
python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_equal_treatment.py -v
```

Two members make the same timely application under the same rule. Both incur zero penalty. The affiliate is denied the open hearing. An earlier affiliate admission contradicts the office's historical claim. The intake office could have confined its ruling or sent the matter for a new hearing. Directly restoring admission belonged to another office. At time 7 an independent-controller label accompanies a successful check of the posted notice. The member's access remains unverified, the follow-up date has passed, and the supplied register contains another matched denial. The tests also exercise a successful access remedy, an ordinary eligibility difference, unknown observations, a later-only precedent and a reopened hearing with relief still pending.

## Packet and interpretation

The [complete example](../examples/equal-treatment-review.json) is the input contract for `equal-treatment-review-1`. The Python API is `commonwealth.remediation_v1.equal_treatment.review(packet, as_of=...)`. The CLI reads bounded strict JSON. Reference the saved input by its packet digest to re-examine testimony. Routine output contains IDs, declared labels and findings. Keep identifying records out of public packets.

| Input | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `decision_at` and nested `remediation` | [M1](REMEDIATION_V1.md) validates the same case ID, evidence references, warning chronology and repair checks. In this fixture, a record's time is when it became available to the reviewing office. Later material stays visible as hindsight. |
| `claimant` and `comparator` | A known access result or penalty requires a referenced observation. The same requested option must be identifiable for distinct people in different declared groups. Unknown facts or mismatched conduct, qualifications, rule version or opportunity make the comparison inconclusive. Even two matched denials can leave both people worse off. |
| `reasoning` | Grounds are caller classifications. A substantive-reason label only records that one was cited. A universal historical claim can be contradicted by a single available, like-scope counterexample. Missing history leaves the premise unconfirmed. An alternative carries its expected effect, date availability and declared scope. Human review must assess the actual reasons and the credibility of each route. |
| `decision` | The output reports merits issues beyond the supplied delegation and any merits pronounced alongside a dismissal. A conflict remains inspectable. A referral, remand, or favorable declaration alone supplies no evidence of access. |
| `followup` | Owner and due date may be unknown. The deadline is inclusive. A supported late observation retains its date. Other post-decision observations must identify a different person. Matched denials remain visible even when this claimant gains access. Their count has no population denominator. |
| Personal relief | Use the exact M1 criterion `requested-access-available-to-claimant` or `claimant-used-requested-access`. Every M1 check used for positive relief must have a corresponding observation of this person and requested opportunity, after implementation, by a separately declared controller. `available` documents independently verified practical access, while `used` records exercise. An offer to apply again or the posting of a notice belongs under its own criterion. A subsequent passing check cannot erase an adverse observation in the same packet. |

The module reads one curated packet. Its results depend on the submitted attributes, access observations, counterexamples and delegations. A fabricated but internally consistent history can pass. The report supplies no aggregate fairness verdict, inferred motive, authority to execute or finding of systemwide correction. Existing P1, R1, A1 and M1 records keep their schemas and replay behavior. A new review has no effect on an emergency halt, previously delegated action or appeal deadline.
