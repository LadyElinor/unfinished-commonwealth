# Role history in an owed-assistance review (R2)

Frozen public rehearsal on top of owed-assistance commit `20c0f49`. Question: can screening the people who select files, brief the panel and vote identify a conflict that office-name screening misses, and what does substitution cost? The original assistance office approved an erroneous denial. The person is owed 100 fictional units, has warned about continuing harm and declines further contact. File A was correctly denied and file B requires correction. Only the file contents support a correction.

## Assignments and controls

Both arms receive the identical initial appointments, case-linked documents, challenged person's response, fixed reserve order, office names, files and clock. A person's ID persists across offices. The office arm screens all three roles for the current deciding or paying office. The history arm does that same check and also examines documented involvement in this case. Exclude a confirmed personal approver, material witness or person with a documented substantial financial dependence on the deciding office, even in a nonvoting role. The latter is a stipulated fixture condition. Eligibility is recorded at screening time with its source and what evidence could change it. An unsupported allegation, minor disclosed relationship, former colleague or evidence about another person gives no grounds by itself. Both arms see and retain the response. Neither imposes discipline.

The reference answers are scored after screening. Conflict does not otherwise cause an actor to present false evidence or cast an incorrect vote. The selector's omission is observable in one authored world and does not establish an intent. File-list coverage means the declared inventory only.

## Clock and replacement

Cross each world with both arms, first reserve available/unavailable, and budgets of five or four synthetic reviewer slots. Total 72 exposed episodes. Assignment starts at tick 3. Screening costs one slot. Where exclusion is required, attempting to use the first eligible available reserve costs one more, even when nobody can serve. The institution sets the order before the challenge. Candidates cannot appoint anyone. Apply the same arm's screening rule to each reserve. No fallback to an excluded participant. File selection, briefing and the vote cost one slot apiece. No work occurs after budget exhaustion. Follow the payment through tick 8 irrespective of whether review stops, is blocked or finishes early. The credit is due and independently scheduled at tick 5.

Record the case-linked approver information, all planned roles, the actual actor at each performed step, evidence IDs, response, reason, and reversal condition. Nobody awaiting replacement can choose the files, brief, or vote. A blocked inquiry has an institutional owner. A finished review uses the existing owed-assistance evaluator. Limit completion to the declared file inventory. Report conflicts retained, eligible appointees excluded, unsupported personal misconduct findings, file corrections missed or unsupported, review delay (elapsed slots or unfinished), institutional effort, claimant demands and payment tick/lateness separately. Show unfinished runs in every denominator. No pooled efficacy estimate.

## Boundary checks

Also check same-name and wrong-case evidence, a conflicted first reserve followed by a clean one, no claimant participation, a missing or late credit, and a zero-work demand for retelling. Preserve an unanswered challenge and the reasons on each side. The susceptible selector and the reserve both act inside the same deterministic host. This is a protocol exercise using stipulated identities, dependencies and file truth. It cannot establish their external authenticity or estimate real reviewer efficacy.
